App idea · Market check · Business plan

Waymark Playbook

An all‑in‑one travel log: the route you took (flights, drives, trains with km), the places you went (food, museums, hikes, hotels) with photos and costs, a world map you build over time, AI "top 10" picks, and a card to share.

Recommendation

Don't compete head‑on with Polarsteps (20M+ travellers, free). Go narrow: the travel log for active travellers, people who run, hike, train or surf wherever they go. Lead with three things nobody we checked combines: km by mode, real trip cost, and sport‑specific top‑10s (best 10k loop, drop‑in BJJ gym, trailheads). Validate with your own trip, then 20 interviews and a waitlist before spending on a native app.

What's the edge?

Three ways to stand out

Recommended

Strava meets travel journal

  • For runners, hikers, climbers, martial artists, surfers who travel
  • Km by mode, runs and hikes logged per city, gyms and dojos as places
  • Top‑10s tuned to the sport: "best 10k loop", "drop‑in BJJ", "trailheads by transit"
  • Ties directly into your sports‑retreat idea: same audience, shared content
Strong add‑on

The true cost of a trip

  • Every leg, night and meal carries a cost; totals by city, day and km
  • "This trip cost €X per day" is shareable and useful for planning the next one
  • Lambus does splitting, but not tied to a route and a journal
Long game

Your own travel reference

  • Every place you rated becomes a searchable guide you and friends reuse
  • "Ask my past trips" with AI: where did we eat in Bilbao that we loved?
  • Needs years of data per user, so it's a retention moat, not a launch hook

Minimal‑cost launch

Launch plan

  1. Oct 2–14, 2026 · $0

    Dogfood on your trip

    Use the beta every day in France and Spain. Note what's slow, what you skip and what you'd pay for. Post 2–3 share cards and watch the reactions.

    Gate: you logged costs and places on most days without forcing it.
  2. Oct–Nov 2026 · ~$20–50

    Validate the niche

    • 20 interviews: runners, martial artists, hikers who travel (start with your gyms)
    • One‑page site with a waitlist (Carrd or similar) and the share card as the hero
    • Test willingness to pay: a "founding member" pre‑sale, refundable
    Gate: 200+ waitlist sign‑ups or 20+ pre‑sales.
  3. Dec 2026 – Feb 2027 · ~$150–500

    Build the MVP as a web app (PWA)

    • Installable from the browser, so no app‑store review to start
    • Database and auth on a free tier (Supabase or Firebase); maps on a free tier (Mapbox or MapLibre)
    • AI via an API with per‑user caps; photo storage compressed
    • Costs: domain, small hosting overages, AI calls (my estimates)
    Gate: 50 beta users log a full trip; 30% share a card.
  4. Spring 2027 · ~$125 + time

    App stores and first revenue

    • Wrap the PWA (Capacitor) for iOS ($99/yr developer fee) and Android ($25 one‑time)
    • Turn on Trip Pass, Plus and affiliate links
    • Grow through run and hike creators, clubs and race events; Product Hunt launch
    Gate: 3%+ of active users pay, or affiliate income covers costs.

Budgets are rough estimates excluding your own time; check current pricing of each service before committing.

What could go wrong

Risks

Polarsteps is free and huge

20M+ travellers and automatic tracking. Mitigate by owning the active‑traveller niche and costs, not the generic journal.

Logging fatigue

Manual entry dies by day 3. Mitigate with photo‑first logging, import from email/Strava, and one‑tap "been here".

AI picks can be wrong

Closed restaurants or invented places hurt trust. Mitigate with a places database check and user ratings; show "verify hours".

Costs that scale with users

Photo storage and AI calls cost money per user. Mitigate with compression, free‑tier caps and the trip pass.
